<objective>
Close the open hypothesis that both committed `sparse-geo` reports leave hanging: was the
floor-plans-500 "SuperPoint is worse" verdict partly a **voting-mode confound** rather than purely a
backend/keypoint-quality effect?

`docs/reports/sparse-geo-improvement.md` measured SuperPoint only at `translation-2dof` and
`pairwise-4dof` (the two modes a frameless backend can use) against a SIFT baseline at
`single-4dof`. Every delta in that table therefore differs in **two** ways at once: backend *and*
voting mode. `docs/reports/sparse-geo-real-objects-superpoint-spike.md` then ran the missing
same-voting-mode SIFT controls on a *different* domain and found that switching SIFT itself off
`single-4dof` costs **0.055–0.079 F1** and **0.050–0.145 AP** on its own, independent of backend —
big enough to account for a large share of the floor-plans deltas.

This spike runs those same controls on floor-plans-500 itself, which is the only place the
hypothesis can actually be checked.

Purpose: turn a stated hypothesis in two committed reports into a measured verdict, using the exact
val-tune → freeze → test-score protocol that produced the published numbers, so the new cells drop
straight into the published table.

Output: a committed driver in `scripts/`, raw results JSON in this quick task's own `runs/`, and
`docs/reports/sparse-geo-floorplans-voting-mode-confound.md`. **No change to `src/` or `conf/`**:
`backend` stays `sift` and `voting_mode` stays `single-4dof` as shipped defaults regardless of what
this measures. The compute runs on a rented vast.ai CPU box at the user's explicit direction.
</objective>
